## CONTEXT.md
# Resource Broker

The Resource Broker (called "the Broker" throughout this repo) controls who gets the GPU on a home PC that gaming, Plex video transcoding, and Ollama inference all share. Gaming and Plex always win the GPU; the Broker queues, pauses, and resumes inference work around them.

Glossary terms (each has exactly one meaning in this repo): Broker, Contention (gaming/Plex claim on the GPU), Yield (whole-Broker state of not admitting inference — reserved exclusively for the gaming/Plex response, do not conflate with this feature's new "Idle" concept), Preemption (interrupting one in-flight request), Job, Queue, Park/Parked request, Drain burst, Position, Fronting Proxy, Synchronous request, Embed lane, Consumer.

This feature introduces two new terms not previously in CONTEXT.md, documented in requirements.md's Terminology note: **Idle** (a backend instance having received no dispatched request for its configured duration) and **Idle-unload** (freeing that instance's VRAM in response to Idle, via the existing systemctl-based Unloader mechanism). Both are explicitly scoped away from "Yield."
